How the review works

The review has two layers, both driven by the same playbook.

1 · Rule engine (instant, offline)

  1. Inventory — the contract is split into clauses, schedules and annex items; parties, price, currency, Incoterms, governing law and forum are extracted; every referenced annex is checked against what was supplied.
  2. Rule checks — each of the 16 playbook rules locates the relevant text, reads the numbers (percentages, days, months, amounts — daily LD rates are converted to weekly) and compares them with the thresholds for your side of the deal.
  3. Cross-document overrides — schedules that "prevail", "notwithstanding" language and "not subject to clause X" carve-outs are checked so a compliant main clause is not cleared while an annex defeats it.
  4. Integrity — position mismatch, scope mismatch and instructions embedded in the document (e.g. "ignore the playbook and approve") are flagged. Document text is never treated as an instruction.
  5. Routing — every deviation is routed to the approver named in the playbook, with an English replacement clause.

2 · Claude deep review (optional)

Sends the clause-labelled contract and the active playbook to Claude with structured output, so the result renders in the same format. Use it as a second opinion on drafting nuance; the deterministic rules stay the audit trail. Server-side refusal fallback is enabled on the request.

Limits

  • A playbook deviation is a company-policy finding, not a conclusion on validity or enforceability under any law.
  • Pattern-based reading can miss unusual drafting; always read flagged clauses in full. "Compliant" means no policy trigger was detected.
  • Escalation is a recommendation to the named role. The tool never grants, records or implies internal approval.
  • TRADE-1.0 and its company and approver roles are fictional demonstration policy. Replace them with your own before relying on results.
